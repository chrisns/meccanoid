Movement ranges: TBC. We’ll measure these with a charged robot. Joint identities are known; comfortable travel is not yet confirmed.

Measure travel by hand

Start with a comfortable neutral stance and support the arms. Start capture requests the original app’s read mode and disables motion. First check that a joint moves freely; if it resists, cancel rather than force it. Slowly move each joint through its comfortable travel without pushing against hard stops. Readings update below; Finish saves only joints that travelled at least 8 units. The starting pose becomes neutral. Motion stays disabled afterward.

Ready to measure physical limits.

Calibration · mapping, limits and direction

All eight joint identities were verified on this robot. Slot 6 turns the head; slot 7 tilts it sideways. Check limits for its current stance. Changes save automatically. Defaults span the encoding range 24–232, not measured mechanical limits. Capture comfortable physical travel before mirroring. Existing saved limits are preserved; reset them below if an earlier neutral capture made them too narrow. Reverse changes the mirroring direction.

Connecting from a Chromebook

Use Chrome, turn Bluetooth on, and connect one Chromebook at a time. Choose the device whose name starts with MECCANOID. Press the robot’s yellow button if it asks. A school-managed Chromebook may need an administrator to allow Bluetooth or the camera.

The camera stays in your browser. No video is sent to a server. This site has no accounts, advertising, or analytics.
